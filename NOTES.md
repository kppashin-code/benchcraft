## The idea

Benchcraft: An AI apprenticeship notebook that teaches PhD students scientific judgement, not just faster experiment production.

This is going to be useful to me every single week of my Oxford DPhil.

The question is crucial:

Can AI help a PhD student be a better scientist, without quietly becoming the scientist for them?

This is more original, and more personally useful for me, than a generic research copilot.

## Benchcraft's function

Benchcraft builds a longitudinal record of the evolution of my scientific judgement across experiments.

I would rather follow a structured research cycle than open a chatbot and ask "What does this result mean?"

### 1. Form your own opinion first

I write, while the AI is waiting:

What did I think?
But what did I really see?
What do I believe happened?
How certain am I?
What would convince me to change my mind?
What is the next experiment going to be?

Initially the system does not show what it thinks.

### 2. Capture implicit bench knowledge

After an experiment I can dictate a 60 sec voice note:

"The slow-relaxing gel was more viscous than the previous batch. Organoids were a bit trickier to centre. Line three was unhealthy before encapsulation."

Benchcraft connects these observations to the right cell line, passage number, hydrogel batch, differentiation day, mechanical condition, reagent lot, protocol version, imaging or sequencing output.

This is important because much of what influences the success of an experiment never ends up in the final spreadsheet.

### 3. Challenge instead of answer

When I settle on my interpretation, the AI is an adversarial partner. It indicates:

three plausible explanations; evidence for and against each; potential confounders; missing controls; the cheapest discriminating experiment; literature that would change the interpretation.

It is instructed to preserve uncertainty rather than collapse everything into one polished conclusion.

### 4. Develop a research decision tree

Over time, Benchcraft builds a visual map:

Question, hypothesis, experiment, protocol deviation, observation, result, interpretation, decision, next experiment.

This allows me to see:

why I abandoned a line of research; when a hypothesis changed; what conclusions are based on a single cell line; whether I always miss a certain confounder; which results are consistent across batches; where my interpretation differs from the AI; which questions are truly open.

### 5. Turn the rationale built up into PhD products

Since the logic is already there, Benchcraft is able to produce:

weekly supervisor meeting briefs; lab meeting slides; experiment plans; protocol-difference tables; troubleshooting records; methods-section drafts; figure-specific interpretation notes; literature matrices; progress-report material; thesis chapter evidence maps.

It doesn't start with the conclusion. It gives me a first draft of my documented reasoning that I can work with.
